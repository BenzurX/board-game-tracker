The mark is the same drawing in all four: the die - plate plus its four plum corner pips - turns slowly about the centre pip, which stays put because it is also the dot of the i, and cycles through the eight player accents. The spin and the colour clocks are deliberately different lengths (7s and 8s), so the pairing never repeats on a short loop. What varies between variants is what surrounds it.

A - Wordmark, nothing else

The full PiP wordmark on the ambient ground, with one micro-label underneath. Tradeoff: the quietest and the closest to the home screen it hands off to, so the transition is nearly invisible - but with no progress cue at all, a slow connection reads as a frozen screen.

B - Mark alone, big, name set below

Letters stripped out of the SVG so the die is the whole image at 250px, with PIP set underneath in the display face and three pulsing dots for life. Tradeoff: the die reads as a die at this size, which the 168px wordmark never quite does - but dropping the letters from the mark means the splash and the home header are two different lockups.

C - Carded, with a sweep bar

The mark sits in the same surface card the modals and the board use, over a looping sweep bar. Tradeoff: the most explicitly "loading" of the four and the most reassuring on a slow connection, but the card is a box the app then throws away, so the hand-off to the home screen is a visible cut rather than a fade.

D - Halo, with rotating tips

A blurred glow behind the mark tracks the pip's colour, so the whole screen changes hue on the same clock, with a game tip fading in and out below. Tradeoff: the most characterful and it makes a long load feel intentional - but the halo is a lot of colour for a screen that should be gone in under a second, and the tips need writing and maintaining.
